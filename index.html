Human Heart and its Mechanical Twin - Diaphragm Pump

See how the human heart and a mechanical diaphragm pump move fluids through repeated changes in pressure.

01 / Biological pump

❤️ The Human Heart

A muscular, four-chambered organ that continuously circulates blood through the lungs and the rest of the body.

Animation: DrJanaOfficial · Wikimedia Commons

How it works — 6 key steps

  1. Oxygen-poor blood returns from the body through the venae cavae and enters the right atrium.
  2. The right atrium contracts, sending blood through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle.
  3. The right ventricle contracts and pumps blood through the pulmonary valve and pulmonary arteries to the lungs.
  4. In the lungs, blood releases carbon dioxide, takes in oxygen, and returns through the pulmonary veins to the left atrium.
  5. Blood passes through the mitral valve into the left ventricle, which contracts to pump it through the aortic valve into the aorta.
  6. The heart alternates relaxation (diastole) and contraction (systole); one-way valves prevent backward flow.
02 / Mechanical pump

⚙️ The Diaphragm Pump

A positive-displacement pump that uses a flexible membrane to draw in and push out liquid or gas.

Animation: TheresNoTime · Wikimedia Commons

How it works — 6 key steps

  1. A flexible diaphragm forms one wall of a sealed pumping chamber.
  2. A mechanical or pneumatic drive pulls the diaphragm back, increasing the chamber volume.
  3. This expansion lowers pressure in the chamber, drawing fluid through the inlet check valve (suction stroke).
  4. The drive pushes the diaphragm forward, decreasing the chamber volume and increasing pressure.
  5. The outlet check valve opens to discharge fluid; the inlet valve closes to stop reverse flow.
  6. Repeated suction and discharge strokes deliver fluid, while the diaphragm separates it from the drive mechanism.

What connects them? Both use cyclic pressure changes and one-way valves to move fluids in a controlled direction. Unlike a mechanical diaphragm pump, the heart uses coordinated muscular contractions and has four chambers.
